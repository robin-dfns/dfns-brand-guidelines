Why we're evolving our brand identity

Where we come from

Dfns today

Technical, developer-first, security-obsessed. Our startup DNA got us here. We built a powerful, deeply technical product with a visual identity rooted in blockchain culture.

That identity served us well as a dev-oriented, security-focused infrastructure provider. Powerful in substance, but complex in expression.

Startup Developer-first Security-obsessed Blockchain-native Powerful Complex

A 3D isometric cube with three interlocking layers — a metaphor for depth and layers of security.
Effective but complex.

The challenge

Breaking the
glass ceiling

And to take it to the next level, we have to say: we're no longer just a technology provider. We want to cross the threshold into the world of institutional finance — and be taken seriously at that table.

To get there, our brand must reflect who we're becoming: a company that institutions trust with their most critical operations. Not a startup selling to developers, but an institution serving institutions.

What guided the change

Two drivers

Simplicity

Strip away complexity in every touchpoint. Our product abstracts blockchain infrastructure — our brand should do the same with our communication. Clear, direct, effortless.

Institutional

Look and sound like a company that belongs in boardrooms, not hackathons. Authority, structure, trust — the standards banks and financial institutions expect from a partner.

"Our product is bank-grade. Our brand needs to match."

The shift

Today, our brand behaves like a small player. The goal is to become the institution that institutions trust.

This applies to image, but also to words. We are not a "WaaS." That term reflects the immaturity of today's products for the finance of tomorrow. It's a band-aid because no accurate translation exists yet.

The symbol

The hexagon

Molecular structure

The hexagon is the most stable and efficient shape found in nature (honeycombs, crystals, graphene). It conveys structural strength without having to say it.

Security

In tech and military iconography, the hexagon is associated with shields, secure networks, and nodes. It is the shape used for security badges, certifications, and "trust marks."

Distributed network

A hexagonal tiling is the only regular tiling where each cell touches 6 equidistant neighbors. Unlike the triangle (unstable) or the square (rigid, bureaucratic), the hexagon evokes a closed yet interconnected system. Perfect for "infrastructure."

We are the Ruler; the hexagon is a geometric form of authority — orderly, symmetrical, imposing a framework. It is order created from chaos, exactly the role of the Ruler.

DNA

From cube to hexagon

Vault

The frame's shape evokes a secure container, a protected enclosure.

Gateway

The central negative space forms an arrow. This perfectly illustrates Dfns' positioning: "the most intuitive, powerful, and secure gateway to the blockchain world."

From 3D to 2D

The evolution from the cube to the flat hexagon is a sign of brand maturity. The cube said, "Look at our complex technology." The hexagon says, "Our technology is so mastered that it boils down to a pure form." It's the transition from demonstration to earned trust.

The interior cutout isn't just an arrow — it creates a sense of perspective or forward motion, like a cube viewed from a slight angle. A subtle echo of the 3D volume of the old cube, without retaining its complexity.

The hexagon isn't original. It's a logo that inspires trust, not seduction.

The letters are custom-drawn, not a standard typeface. Geometric and structured — straight lines, no frills. Sharp and clean, with controlled curves and flat horizontal terminals. No serifs, no excessive roundness — a geometric sans-serif with weight.

Typography

From Dfns to DFNS

All our partners and customers call us DFNS. Very few call us Defense.
Defense is where it all began. Today:

Pure authority

The capital letters are set in Ruler typeface. Financial institutions, law firms, central banks: HSBC, NYSE, DTCC. In all caps, Dfns moves beyond the startup realm and enters the realm of financial infrastructure.

A self-assured acronym

"Dfns" in mixed case poses a readability issue: how is it pronounced? In all caps, DFNS reads like an acronym, not a word. This eliminates cognitive friction.

Monolith

The four capital letters have identical heights, creating a compact, uniform block. This visually reinforces the metaphor of a rampart, a secure wall.
